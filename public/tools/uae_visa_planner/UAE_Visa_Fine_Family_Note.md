# The UAE Visa Fine: What Happened and How to Avoid It Again

A note for the family
10 August 2026

---

## The short version

Mummy and Papa were fined AED 1,015 each when they flew back on 15 February 2026. The reason was the **annual** limit on their five-year visa, not because any single trip was too long.

Here is the part that catches everyone out. The visa gives **90 days a year**, not 180. The 180-day figure that appears in every news article is what you can reach *only if you apply and pay for an extension*. Without that application, the allowance stays at 90.

In the twelve months before they flew out, they had spent 105 days in the UAE. That is 15 days over the limit. The receipt says "Immigration Over Stay, 15 DAYS." Everything matches.

So the fine was correct. The rule is simply stricter than the headline number suggests.

---

## How the 15 days added up

The four trips so far:

| Trip | In the UAE | Days |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 Oct 2024 to 10 Nov 2024 | 38 |
| 2 | 12 Mar 2025 to 20 Apr 2025 | 39 |
| 3 | 17 Oct 2025 to 25 Nov 2025 | 39 |
| 4 | 20 Jan 2026 to 15 Feb 2026 | 27 |

The trap is that the "year" is a **rolling twelve months**. It is not a calendar year and not a fixed block. It moves forward every single day.

On 15 February 2026, the system looked back twelve months to 16 February 2025. Trip 1 had already fallen outside that window, so it did not count. Trips 2, 3 and 4 were all inside it:

**39 + 39 + 27 = 105 days**

Minus the 90-day allowance leaves 15 days of overstay.

You can pinpoint the exact day it went wrong. When they landed on 20 January 2026, trips 2 and 3 had already used 78 of their 90 days. That left 12 days, which ran out on 31 January. Every day from 1 February to 15 February was an overstay. Fifteen days.

Both parents travelled on identical dates, so both received identical fines.

---

## The one number that does not add up

Fifteen days at the standard rate of AED 50 per day comes to **AED 750**. They were charged **AED 1,015** each. That leaves **AED 265** unexplained per person, or AED 530 between them.

The VAT line gives a clue. VAT was only AED 5.54 across both people. At 5%, that means only about AED 110 of the total was a taxable service charge, roughly AED 55 each. Government fines do not carry VAT, so everything else was treated as a government charge.

That leaves around AED 210 per person nobody has accounted for. It is not an exit permit fee, since those only apply to overstays beyond 30 days. This is worth querying with ICP.

---

## Do not trust the "39 days remaining" on the ICP website

The file validity page currently shows 66 days used and 39 days remaining.

But 90 minus 66 is **24**, not 39. The website is overstating the available days by exactly 15, which happens to be the same as the overstay they were just fined for. It looks as though the system credited those days back once the fine was paid, but that is a guess.

**Plan around 24 days, not 39.** Booking a 39-day trip on the strength of that screen risks a second fine.

---

## Planning the next visit

The 39 days from trip 3 only drop out of the rolling window on **25 November 2026**. Until then, the available days stay tight.

**Now until late November 2026**
Keep any trip to roughly 20 days, leaving a safety margin.

**From December 2026 onwards**
Around 63 days become available again as trip 3 falls out of the window.

**The rule to follow every single time**
Before booking anything, add up all the days spent in the UAE over the previous twelve months. Add the days of the trip being planned. Make sure the total stays under 90.

It is worth keeping a simple running list of every entry and exit date. That one habit would have prevented this fine entirely.

---

## One thing to confirm before booking

GDRFA's published wording says a visa becomes invalid if the holder breaches the permitted stay period, with no refund.

The ICP page currently shows the file status as CLOSED. That is most likely just the normal state for someone who is outside the country. But given the overstay, it is worth getting ICP to confirm in writing that both visas remain usable until they expire in October 2029, before anyone books flights.

Call **600 522 222**. Have the file numbers and unified numbers ready.

## Where these rules come from

The 90-day allowance, the option to extend to 180, the rolling year starting from first entry, and the rule that unused days expire all come from GDRFA's published requirements of July 2026, as reported by Gulf News and Khaleej Times.

The AED 50 per day fine has applied across all seven emirates and all visa types since 11 February 2026, when ICP unified the previously varying rates. There is no grace period for visit visas.

One caveat worth noting. This guidance comes from news reporting of GDRFA and ICP announcements rather than a single official rulebook, and UAE visa rules changed twice during 2026. Whatever ICP confirms in writing should be treated as the final word over anything in this note.
