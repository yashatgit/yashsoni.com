How the one-year window works

On any day in the UAE, the limit looks back exactly one year from that day. The shaded band is that year. Trips inside it count, and trips that slide out of it stop counting. Drag the band to see the count on other days.

The rules, in plain English

1. You get 90 days a year, not 180.

The 180-day figure in every headline is only reachable through a separate, paid extension applied for inside the UAE before the first 90 days run out. Without that application, the ceiling stays at 90.

Sources: Gulf News — 5-year visa explained · ICP — visa extension service

2. The year rolls. It never resets.

On any day you are in the UAE, the system looks back one year to the same date and counts every day you spent inside. It is not a calendar year and not the visa anniversary. Each day you spend stops counting a year later. Three ordinary trips can add up past 90 inside one year, which is how the 15-day fine in February 2026 happened even though no single trip was long.

Sources: Gulf News — year counted from first entry, not calendar year · Khaleej Times — 5-year visa rules

3. Entry day and exit day both count.

Land at 11:50 pm and that whole day is counted. Fly out at 6 am and that day counts too. A “two-week” trip is 15 counted days if you travel on both weekend ends. This page counts the same way the fine receipt did.

Verified against the actual fine receipt — the arithmetic only matches when both travel days are counted.

4. Overstay costs AED 50 a day, from day one.

Since 11 February 2026 the fine is a flat AED 50 per day across all seven emirates and all visa types. Visit and tourist visas get no grace period — the meter starts the first day over.

Sources: VisaHQ — unified AED 50/day fine · inkl — overstay fines 2026 explained

5. Check ICP's screen too, and go with the earlier date.

ICP's File Validity screen shows days spent and days remaining. For this file they always add up to 105, not 90, probably because the 15 fined days were added back. Its remaining days are a plain subtraction, so its leave-by date can be earlier than this planner's. Enter ICP's number at the top of this page and the planner uses whichever date comes first.

Check yourself: ICP File Validity · GDRFA Dubai · ICP call centre 600 522 222
Caveat: UAE visa rules changed twice during 2026, and the guidance above comes from news reporting of GDRFA and ICP announcements rather than a single official rulebook. Whatever ICP confirms in writing overrides anything on this page.
