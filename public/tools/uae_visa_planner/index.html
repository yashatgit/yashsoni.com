The rules, in plain English

1. You get 90 days a year, not 180.

The 180-day figure in every headline is only reachable through a separate, paid extension applied for inside the UAE before the first 90 days run out. Without that application, the ceiling stays at 90.

Sources: Gulf News — 5-year visa explained · ICP — visa extension service

2. The year rolls. It never resets.

The 90 days are measured over the 365 days behind any given day you are in the country — not a calendar year, not the visa anniversary. Each day you spend only comes back exactly 365 days later. Three modest trips can quietly stack past 90 inside one moving window, which is precisely how a 15-day overstay happened to us without any single trip being too long.

Sources: Gulf News — year counted from first entry, not calendar year · Khaleej Times — 5-year visa rules

3. Entry day and exit day both count.

Land at 11:50 pm and that whole day is counted. Fly out at 6 am and that day counts too. A “two-week” trip is 15 counted days if you travel on both weekend ends. This page counts the same way the fine receipt did.

Verified against the actual fine receipt — the arithmetic only matches when both travel days are counted.

4. Overstay costs AED 50 a day, from day one.

Since 11 February 2026 the fine is a flat AED 50 per day across all seven emirates and all visa types. Visit and tourist visas get no grace period — the meter starts the first day over.

Sources: VisaHQ — unified AED 50/day fine · inkl — overstay fines 2026 explained

5. Keep your own count. Don’t trust the dashboard.

The ICP file-validity page has shown us 15 more remaining days than the arithmetic allows — exactly the days that were fined. Keep your own entry/exit list (this page), plan on the lower number, and get anything important confirmed by ICP in writing.

Check yourself: ICP Smart Services · GDRFA Dubai · ICP call centre 600 522 222
