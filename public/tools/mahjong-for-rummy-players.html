Mahjong for Rummy Players

Simple Chinese / Hong Kong style, with no scoring. Enough to play your first game tonight.

The short version: it's rummy with tiles. You hold 13, draw one and discard one each turn. The first player to finish 4 sets + 1 pair (14 tiles) calls "Mahjong!" and wins.

2The tiles

Dots (circles) · 36 tiles

  • Numbers 1 to 9, 4 copies of each. Count the rings to read the number.
  • 1 dot is the one big flower-shaped circle.
  • Can make runs, pungs, kongs and pairs.

Bamboo (sticks)

  • 36 tiles: numbers 1 to 9, 4 copies of each. Count the sticks.
  • 1 bamboo is the bird. 8 bamboo is the W over an M. The red sticks are only decoration.
  • Can make runs, pungs, kongs and pairs.

Characters

  • 36 tiles: numbers 1 to 9, 4 copies of each.
  • Read the dark symbol on top. 一 二 三 are 1, 2, 3 lines. 伍 is 5 in your set. The red 萬 below is the same on every tile and just marks the suit.
  • Can make runs, pungs, kongs and pairs.

  • "Identical" means exactly the same tile: 5 dots with 5 bamboo is not a pair.
  • A pair in your hand can stay a pair, or become a pung if the third tile turns up. Keeping two or three pairs early gives you options.
  • Your booklet calls the pair "the head of the bird". If your 4 sets are done and you hold one single tile, only its exact match can win you the game.
  • With a kong in your hand, a winning hand has 15 tiles (4 + 3 + 3 + 3 + 2). That's fine.

4Starting a game

Step 1: Sort the tiles

Take out the flowers, seasons and blank spares. That leaves 136 tiles. Turn them all face down and mix them around on the table with both hands. This is the "shuffle".

Step 2: Sit and pick the dealer

Each player rolls the two dice. The highest roll is the dealer, called East. The others take their seat names going anticlockwise from East: the player on East's right is South, then West opposite, then North on East's left.

Play always moves anticlockwise: East → South → West → North → East. So "the next player" is the one on your right.

Step 3: Build the wall

Each player builds their own row in front of them, face down: 17 stacks long, each stack 2 tiles high (34 tiles). Then everyone pushes their row forward so the four rows meet and form a square. This square is the wall, your stock pile.

Step 4: Break the wall

  1. East rolls both dice and adds them up, say 7.
  2. Count around the table anticlockwise, starting with East as 1: East 1, South 2, West 3, North 4, East 5, South 6, West 7. So the break happens in West's row.
  3. West counts 7 stacks from the right end of their row and pulls the wall apart there.

Want to keep it simple? Skip the dice and let East start taking tiles from any corner. The game plays the same.

Step 5: Deal

  1. Starting at the break, East takes the next 2 stacks (4 tiles). Then South takes the next 2 stacks, then West, then North.
  2. Go round like this 3 times, so everyone has 12 tiles.
  3. Then each player takes 1 more tile, and East takes 1 extra. East now has 14, everyone else has 13.
  4. Tiles always come off the wall in one direction (clockwise around the square), so the wall shrinks from one end.

Stand your tiles up facing you, like holding cards, and sort them by suit.

Step 6: East starts

East already has 14 tiles, so East does not draw. If East's 14 tiles already form a winning hand (rare), East wins straight away. Otherwise East discards one tile, and the game is running.

5How the turns go

A normal turn

  1. Draw the next tile from the wall. You now have 14.
  2. If those 14 tiles make 4 sets + 1 pair, call "Mahjong!" and show your hand. You win.
  3. If not, discard one tile face up into the middle and say its name out loud, for example "five bamboo".
  4. Pause a second so others can claim it. Then the player on your right takes their turn.

Just like rummy, you always end your turn holding 13 tiles.

After every discard, the table checks

  1. Can anyone win with it? They call "Mahjong!" and take it.
  2. Does anyone have a pair of it? They can call "Pung!" from any seat.
  3. Does the next player have two tiles that make a run with it? Only they can call "Chow!"
  4. Nobody wants it? The next player draws from the wall as normal.

Whoever claims the tile lays the finished set face up, discards one tile, and play continues to their right. Anyone in between is skipped.

Example of a few turns

  1. East discards 3 dots. South holds 4 and 5 dots, calls "Chow!", lays 3-4-5 dots face up, then discards 9 bamboo.
  2. North holds two 9 bamboo, calls "Pung!" even though it's West's turn. West is skipped. North lays 9-9-9 bamboo face up and discards South wind.
  3. Nobody wants South wind. Play moves to North's right, so East draws from the wall and discards.
  4. It keeps going round like this until someone calls "Mahjong!"

Kong (four of a kind)

  • From a discard: you hold three, someone discards the fourth. Call "Kong!", lay all four face up.
  • All four drawn yourself: on your turn, lay them face down (or show them) to declare the kong.
  • Adding to your pung: if you have an exposed pung and draw the fourth, add it.

Every kong uses 4 tiles for one set, so draw a replacement tile from the far end of the wall, then discard as usual. Your booklet calls a kong from a discard a "Ming-kong" (visible) and one you drew yourself an "Em-kong" (hidden).

Should you call kong?

You're never forced to. Your three tiles are already a valid set, so weigh it up:

You gainYou give up
A free extra draw from the wallEveryone sees the set and knows that tile is gone
Your turn comes sooner (section 6)Those tiles can't be used in runs any more
More points, once you add scoring

Simple rule: call it when your hand is still messy. Skip it when you're close to winning and want to keep quiet, or when those tiles fit runs you're building.

How a hand ends

  • Someone wins: they call "Mahjong!" on their own draw or on anyone's discard, and lay all 14 tiles face up so everyone can check.
  • The wall runs out: nobody wins. The hand is a draw.

The next hand

If East won (or it was a draw), East deals again. If anyone else won, the dealer role moves to the right: South becomes the new East, and everyone's seat name shifts. Mix the tiles, rebuild the wall and go again.

6Grabbing discards (the big change from rummy)

Each discard can be claimed for a short moment, until the next player draws. Who can take it depends on what you'll use it for:

To make…Who can claim it
Mahjong (it finishes your hand)Anyone. This always wins the tile.
Pung or KongAnyone, even out of turn. Play jumps to you.
Chow (a run)Only the player next in turn, the one to the discarder's right.

When you claim, take the tile, put that finished set face up in front of you, then discard. Play continues from your seat, so people in between lose their turn.

Sets made from tiles you drew yourself stay hidden in your hand. Only sets made with a claimed discard go face up. Keep everything else standing up, facing you.

Only the latest discard

You can only claim the tile that was just thrown, and only before the next player draws. After that it's dead: it stays in the middle for the rest of the hand and nobody can take it.

Who loses a turn when you claim

Turns go East → South → West → North. Say you're North and East discards a tile you pung:

  • Without claiming: South plays, then West, then you.
  • With pung or kong: you play straight away, then East. South and West lose their turn.

7Playing smarter

What to throw away first

  1. Lone winds. A single wind only helps if two more of that exact wind turn up.
  2. Lone dragons, especially ones others have already discarded. Keep a dragon once you have two.
  3. Lone 1s and 9s. They fit only one run each (1-2-3 or 7-8-9).
  4. Keep middle numbers (3 to 7). A 5 can join 3-4-5, 4-5-6 or 5-6-7, or become a pair.

Count what's left

Every tile exists 4 times. Before waiting on a tile, count how many you can see in discards and face-up sets.

  • Example: you hold 7-8 bamboo, waiting for 6 or 9. Three 6 bamboo are in someone's pung, so only one 6 is left. Hope for the 9.
  • You can't see other players' hidden tiles, so you never know for sure. If your wait isn't coming after several turns, rebuild around a different one.
  • If nobody has thrown a certain suit for a while, someone is probably collecting it. Don't throw it to them.

Getting value from honours

  • Pairs of honours are worth holding. Others throw honours early, so the third often comes, and anyone can pung it.
  • They make a clean pair for your winning hand.
  • They're safe late discards. If two East winds are already out, a third is unlikely to give someone the win, because nobody can use it in a run.
  • Without scoring they're mostly junk. Add the simple scoring in section 10 and they start to matter.

8What trips up rummy players

  • No runs with honours. East-South-West is not a run. Winds and dragons only make pungs, kongs and pairs.
  • Runs stay in one suit. 3 dots, 4 bamboo, 5 dots is not a chow.
  • Exactly one pair. Not zero, not two.
  • No jokers and no laying off tiles on other people's sets.
  • Discards are public. Look at what others throw away and which sets they've laid down. It tells you what they don't need.
  • Don't claim everything. Every exposed set shows the table what you're building and leaves you fewer options.
  • No claiming a discard for a pair, unless it's the tile that wins you the hand.
  • Don't lay half-built runs face up. Only completed sets made with a claimed discard go on the table.

"Mahjong!"

10House rules

Playing with 3

  • Easiest: same rules, nobody sits at North. Still build 4 walls, deal 13 each (14 to East). Hands take a bit longer.
  • Faster option: remove characters 2 to 8 (keep 1 and 9) before shuffling. Many groups also stop allowing chow from discards.

Simple scoring

Keep a tally on paper over several hands:
